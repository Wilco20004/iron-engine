Gym words, in plain English

Rep
One full movement, for example lowering the bar to your chest and pushing it back up once.
Set
A group of reps done without stopping. "4 × 6–8" means 4 sets of 6 to 8 reps each.
Rest
How long to sit or walk around between sets before starting the next one.
RIR
Reps in reserve: how many more reps you could still have done. RIR 2 means stop when you have about 2 left in the tank.
Fail (F)
You kept going until you couldn't do another clean rep. Tick Fail on that set; it shows as "8F" in your log.
Load
The weight you used, in kg. Write it down so you know what to beat next time.
Superset
Two exercises done back to back (marked A1 and A2), then you rest. Saves time on arm work.
Barbell
The long bar you load plates on. A standard Olympic bar weighs 20 kg on its own.
Dumbbell
A short handheld weight, one in each hand.
EZ-bar
A short zig-zag barbell that is easier on the wrists for curls.
Cable
A machine with a pulley and a stack of weights. You clip on a handle or rope.
Hinge
Bending at the hips with a flat back, like closing a car door with your bum. Deadlifts are hinges.
Warm-up set
A light practice set before your real sets. It doesn't count toward the sets in the table.
Zone 2
Easy cardio where you can still talk in full sentences. Builds fitness without tiring your muscles.
HIIT
High-intensity intervals: short hard bursts followed by easy recovery, repeated.
Max HR
Your highest heart rate, roughly 220 minus your age. A watch or chest strap shows your live heart rate.
Deload
A planned easy week so your body can recover and come back stronger.
